3.1    Correlation and convolution (10 points)
      Image filtering is a fundamental tool of image processing. Refer to the lecture materials or Chapter
3 of Szeliski’s Computer Vision: Algorithms and Applications (2nd edition) for further reading.
      Implement cross_correlation_2d without flipping the kernel.
      Implement convolve_2d by flipping both kernel axes and calling your correlation function.


3.2    Gaussian blur and low-pass filtering (10 points)
      Low pass filters are used to remove high-frequency details from an image, making it appear
smoother. You can create a low pass filter by applying a Gaussian blur, which computes a weighted
average of neighboring pixels.
      Implement gaussian_blur_kernel_2d for a given odd size k and positive standard deviation σ.
      Implement low_pass using this kernel and your convolution function.


3.3    Subsampling (5 points)
      Subsampling reduces the resolution of an image by discarding rows and columns. Given an image
of dimensions M × N , subsampling by a factor of s will result in a new image with dimensions (M /s) ×
(N /s), where M, N should be divisible by s.
      Implement a simple downsampling method: discard every alternate row and column to produce an
image that is half the size of the original.


3.4    Gaussian pyramid (20 points)
      A Gaussian pyramid is a multi-scale representation of an image, where each level is progressively
blurred and downsampled. At each level of the pyramid, the image is smoothed using a Gaussian filter
and then downsampled to reduce its resolution.
      Implement a four-level pyramid: the original image and images at approximately 1/2, 1/4, and 1/8
of its width and height.


3.5    Filtered vs direct subsampling (10 points)
      Use a textured image as the input to the Gaussian pyramid in the previous part. From the same
input, build a second four-level pyramid by directly downsampling each level by a factor of two, without
Gaussian filtering or other smoothing. Compare the corresponding levels of the two pyramids. Report
whether they differ, describe any visible differences, and explain the reasons, including possible aliasing.
